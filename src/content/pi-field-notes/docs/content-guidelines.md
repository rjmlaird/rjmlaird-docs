# Content guidelines

How to write for this site. The aim is clear, accurate and kind to the reader.

## Voice

- Write in the first person for your own work: "I tried", "I chose".
- Be clear, precise and friendly. Concise technical prose beats hype.
- Avoid marketing language. Do not call something "powerful", "seamless" or "game-changing".
- Say what you know and how you know it. Say "I have not measured this" when that is true.

## Spelling and style

- Use UK English: colour, behaviour, analyse, metre, grey, licence (noun) and license (verb).
- Use sentence case for headings.
- Write dates as `11 October 2026` in prose and `2026-10-11` in front matter.
- Use SI units, with a space between number and unit: `5 V`, `2.5 W`, `32 GB`.
- Spell out acronyms on first use: "all-sky camera (ASC)".

## Honesty rules

1. **Do not fabricate results.** No invented measurements, images, prices, benchmarks or completed builds.
2. **Mark the state.** Make clear what is a plan, what is a test and what is a result.
3. **Date your numbers.** Prices and software versions change. Record when you noted them.
4. **Record failures.** Dead ends and mistakes belong in the log, not only in your notes.
5. **Separate fact from opinion.** "The sensor reads 12% high against the reference" is a fact; "it is not good enough" is a judgement.
6. **Credit others.** Link to sources, tutorials, datasets and people whose work you used.

## Structure

- Lead with what the reader most needs. One idea per paragraph.
- Use headings so people can scan, and lists for steps and parts.
- Give commands in code blocks that can be copied as they are.
- Explain why, not only what. A configuration without its reason cannot be adapted.

## Accessibility

- Every meaningful image has descriptive alt text.
- Link text makes sense out of context. Write "the Raspberry Pi documentation", not "click here".
- Do not rely on colour alone to carry meaning in diagrams.
- Keep tables for data that has rows and columns, and give them header rows.
- Do not use headings only for their size. Keep the order logical, with no skipped levels.

## Privacy and safety

- Do not publish passwords, API keys, tokens or full network details. Use placeholders.
- Do not publish your home address or precise coordinates. Round or describe the area instead.
- Think before publishing images that show other people, number plates or house numbers.
- Do not recommend exposing a Raspberry Pi directly to the internet. Prefer a VPN, a tunnel or a one-way upload.
- Mains electricity and batteries can hurt people. Where a build involves them, say what is safe and what needs a qualified person.

## Licensing

- Written content and diagrams are CC BY-SA 4.0. Do not paste in text or images you do not have the right to share.
- Code snippets in the repository are MIT licensed.
- Check the licence on any dataset, and give the attribution it requires.
