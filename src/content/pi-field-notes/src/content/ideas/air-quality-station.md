## The idea

Build a small Raspberry Pi station that logs particulate matter and, possibly, nitrogen dioxide or other gases at one spot, then compare it with nearby official monitors.

## Why it might be worth doing

Official stations are accurate but sparse. A cheap sensor can show local differences, such as the effect of a busy road or a bonfire, even if its absolute values are less reliable.

## Things to find out

- How well low-cost particulate sensors agree with reference instruments, and in what conditions they drift or mislead, for example in high humidity.
- Which gas sensors are usable at home, and which are not worth the money.
- Where to mount the sensor so it samples ordinary air rather than its own enclosure.
- How to present uncertainty honestly, so a noisy reading is not mistaken for a measurement.

## First step

Read the published evaluations of the sensors under consideration, then write a short note on what the data could and could not support.
